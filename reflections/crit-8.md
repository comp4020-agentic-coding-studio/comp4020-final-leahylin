# Crit 8 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

At first, the idea was close to a small Meetup clone. The breakthrough came when I asked the agent, **“Once people have agreed to meet, what actually gets them from the app to the meetup?”** That question moved my focus from features to whether a meetup actually happens. It gave me a working definition of good: the app should shorten the gap between wanting company and turning up. Most of my later decisions follow from it: meetups must be concrete and near, joining and leaving take one action, the head count is real, and an edit says exactly what changed.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

Early on, the agent wrote the whole app in one go and committed it under my
name. It worked, and the tests passed, but I couldn't say which decisions
were mine. Undoing those commits and going through the work piece by piece
was slower. But it's the only reason I can now explain every choice in the
app, from the name to why the head count can't be lowered below the people
already going.

I want to be a developer who stays the author of the work, even when an
agent writes most of the code. That means deciding what good means before
asking for code, choosing between options instead of accepting the first
one, and reviewing before committing. Speed is easy to get from an agent.
Judgement is the part I have to bring.
